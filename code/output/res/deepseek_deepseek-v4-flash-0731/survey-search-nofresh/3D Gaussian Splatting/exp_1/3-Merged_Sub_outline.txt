### 3.1 Scale-Aware Rendering and Anti-Aliasing
Description: This subsection addresses the fundamental challenge of representing continuous scenes with discrete primitives, which causes aliasing, dilation artifacts, and resolution-dependent inconsistencies in the original 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). It reviews adaptations to the rendering pipeline and primitive parameterization that attain multi-scale consistency, level-of-detail (LOD) control, and artifact-free outputs across varying viewing distances and focal lengths.
1. Frequency-constrained filtering and mip-mapping: Covers methods like Mip-Splatting that introduce 3D smoothing filters to constrain high-frequency signal content of Gaussians relative to the training sampling rate, and replace 2D dilation with 2D Mip filters to suppress aliasing when zooming in or out. Discusses physically-grounded regularizations, such as Pyramidal Multi-Scale Supervision, that force consistency across an image pyramid to prevent flickering and degenerate, jagged primitives.
2. Level-of-detail (LOD) and hierarchical structures: Discusses frameworks that manage rendering complexity and detail across scales, such as Octree-GS, with LOD-structured Gaussians and multi-resolution anchor points to select the appropriate detail level, ensuring consistent rendering speeds and quality across zoom-in and zoom-out views of large, complex scenes.
3. Arbitrary-scale and super-resolution rendering: Explores techniques for rendering at new, unusual resolutions without retraining, using adaptive Gaussian scale regularization and generative priors to maintain coherence and structural consistency between high-resolution renderings and the original low-resolution scene.

### 3.2 Geometric and Surface Regularization for Accurate Reconstruction
Description: This subsection focuses on the inherently loose coupling between Gaussian primitives and surface geometry, which leads to noise, holes, and floaters. It covers strategies that constrain optimization with geometric priors, transform representation for surface alignment, and decouple geometry from appearance for structurally stable reconstruction.

2. Planar-based and surface-aligned primitives: Examines the transition of using 2D Gaussian splats as a stricter planar representation to enforce view consistency and normal constraints, and their subsequent extension into hybrid representations with 2D/3D Gaussian features or surface-aware constraints to facilitate high-accuracy mesh extraction and reconstruction.
3. Avoiding local minima and geometric noise: Addresses the occurrence of 'floater' artifacts due to false local minima and gradient vanishing, through decoupled optimization paths for geometric regularization and appearance refinement, lowering external geometric priors to trap rail, and explicit noise-injection and relocalization schemes, such as SGLD in Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) based frameworks.

2. Physical and material modeling, extending the Gaussian primitives with semantic or physically-based attributes beyond view-dependent color, integrating physically based rendering and material models, allowing for the decomposition of the scene into albedo, normal, and roughness maps for relighting applications and realistic optical effects.

Spatially scalable structures for large-scale scenes, presenting the challenges of centering-specific rendering, and a taxonomy for different loading and streaming memory management, including out-of-core optimization, distributed parallel processing across multiple devices, and level-of-detail hierarchies for managing dynamic memory budgets within main memory and ensuring a consistent real-time visualization of city-scale environments.

Feed-forward and generalizable reconstruction, framing the shift from per-scene optimization and overfitting to learning-based systems with scene generalizability, including the use of deep feed-forward network structures that directly predict Gaussian attributes from a few images, and the integration of these methods within larger frameworks for creation of scalable, large-scale and 3D-Aware content.

### 4.1 Surface Extraction and Geometry Reconstruction
Description: This subsection addresses the transformation of the discrete, view-synthesis-oriented Gaussian cloud into a continuous, watertight, and accurate geometric representation. 1. Surface-aligned primitive adaptation: Examining methods that modify the Gaussian primitive itself, such as flattening them into 2D disks (2D Gaussians) or introducing regularization terms to encourage alignment with the scene's surface, which enforces normal consistency and suppresses "floaters" for improved geometric cohesion.
2. Hybrid representations with explicit geometry: Exploring the fusion of Gaussian splatting with mesh-based structures or voxel grids, where Gaussians are bound to mesh faces or guided by signed distance fields (SDFs) from low-resolution octrees, creating an explicit, learnable surface that facilitates high-quality extraction and physically-based manipulation of the reconstructed scene.
3. Projection-based and neural surface extraction: Describing approaches that leverage the high-quality rendering of the Gaussian model itself, either by rendering novel stereo views for depth estimation and fusing the resulting depth maps, or by employing graph-based or network modules to directly predict a surface from the optimized Gaussian attributes.
4. Multi-primitive and dynamic surface hybrid representations: Exploring methods that jointly optimize a mixture of primitive types, like 2D disks and 3D ellipsoids, to accurately model complex objects with both flat and volumetric parts, and the specific challenges of reconciling a rigid or deformation field mesh with the Gaussian distribution for dynamic surface reconstruction.

### 4.2 Physically-Based Modeling and Material Decomposition
Description: This section details the extension of Gaussian primitives from pure radiance carriers to full physically-based rendering parameters, enabling the disentanglement of scene geometry, material properties, and lighting. Surface-aware inverse rendering frameworks: Discussing the integration of Gaussian splatting with differentiable physically-based rendering (PBR) by incorporating material maps (albedo, normal, roughness) and environment lighting, with methods addressing the challenge of accurate geometry and normal estimation for cohesive light transport and correct material-physical disentanglement.
2. Reflection-aware and specular appearance modeling: Detailing the techniques for representing complex light interactions, such as replacing spherical harmonics with view-dependent opacity, incorporating a microfacet material model with ray tracing, or using per-primitive texture maps to model and separate the high-frequency specular and diffuse components of highly reflective or semi-transparent surfaces.
3. Compact and shared material representation: Outlining the strategies for efficient and editable material modeling, such as using a discrete number of material prototypes and palette-based materials in a shared PBR framework, and material to separate and global attributes, which eases editing and can be embedded for domain-specific applications like relighting.
4. Geometry-Material mutual-boosted optimization: The integration of a surface estimation branch with a radiance branch to jointly perform geometry and appearance reconstruction, where each branch provides supervision and constraints to the other, allowing for material and lighting decomposition to improve the accuracy and fidelity of the final model.

### 4.3 Spatial Scalability and Large-Scale Scene Representation

### 4.4 Compression, Compact Representation, and Storage Optimization
Description: This subsection explores the need to reduce the architectural large memory footprint of a trained Gaussian model. Sensitivity-aware vector quantization and attribute compression: Exploring the application of quantization-aware training to group Gaussian parameters into discrete codebooks, or the use of size-aware and mixed-integer programming to optimize bit-width selection and reduce redundancy, combined with the handling of high-frequency details for a more accurate and bit-efficient representation.

### 5.1 Foundations of Deformable and Canonical-Space Modeling
Description: This subsection establishes the foundational paradigm for dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS), wherein a static canonical set of 3D Gaussians is mapped to per-timestamp configurations through learned deformation fields. 1. Canonical Gaussian Deformation via Temporal Mapping: Introduces the core framework of representing a dynamic scene as a canonical set of 3D Gaussians whose positions, rotations, and attributes are displaced by a time-conditioned deformation field (e.g., an MLP) to reconstruct a specific frame, emphasizing the efficiency gains of amortized temporal modeling.
2. Regression Models for Motion Displacement: Covers the evolution of deformation fields from simple positional encoding-based MLPs (e.g., GauFRe, D-3DGS) to more sophisticated architectures, including HexPlane-encoded features (e.g., 4DGS with deformation fields), with a focus on balancing representational capacity against computational overhead.
3. Continuous-Time and ODE-Based Deformation Fields: Explores recent advances in formulating deformation as continuous-time dynamical systems, including the use of Closed-form Continuous-time cells and Liquid Neural Networks to enforce inherent temporal smoothness and avoid discrete per-frame overfitting, thereby improving time interpolation of the deformation field.
4. Rigidity Constraints and Motion Regularization for Coherent Dynamics: Analyzes the challenges of reconstructing physically-plausible motion, highlighting the methods used to regularize optimizer behavior, such as local-rigidity constraints to preserve local geometry, a ray-based grouping strategy to maintain spatial relationships, and the need for motion prior-based initialization to ensure correct gradient flow and avoid local optima.

### 5.2 Explicit and Native Spatio-Temporal Gaussian Representations
