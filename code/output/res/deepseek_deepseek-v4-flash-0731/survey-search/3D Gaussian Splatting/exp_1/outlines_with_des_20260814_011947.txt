Error-guided and Hierarchical Supervision: Review of loss functions that prioritize perceptually informative pixels or frequency bands, including multi-resolution and pyramid-based supervision schemes that progressively guide the network to improve detail recovery rather than uniformly treating all spatial regions.
4. Algorithmic rendering advancements and level-of-detail strategies: convergent rendering speed through novel rasterization strategies, hierarchical visibility culling, and level-of-detail management that dynamically adjust the number and complexity of primitives based on the viewed region and camera distance, enabling constant frame rate for city-scale or intricate environments.
2. Model compression and data quantization: The reduction of memory overhead using techniques such as vector quantization for visual attributes, importance-based pruning and replanting of high-impact-based Gaussians, entropy coding of parameters, and conversion of the primitive cloud into a more compact latent or grid-based representation to significantly lower the storage and bandwidth requirements.

Hardware-accelerated and software-hardware co-design: Technological solutions that include custom graphic processing unit kernels for faster splat rasterization, specialized hardware accelerators optimized for the sorting and blending operations, and warp-level primitives and resource-aware scheduling to fully exploit the available parallel computing power.

### 3.1 Algorithmic Rendering Acceleration and Level-of-Detail Management
Description: This subsection explores algorithmic innovations that reduce per-frame computational costs and enable scalable rendering of complex scenes. It examines strategies that directly optimize the rasterization pipeline itself, from accelerating the blending process to eliminating redundant computations, and introduces level-of-detail (LoD) techniques that adaptively control primitive count and complexity. The goal is to achieve sustained real-time frame rates by attacking the computational bottlenecks of per-pixel blending, per-Gaussian processing, and depth-based sorting, particularly for large-scale environments.
1. Novel rasterization and blending schemes: Investigation of sorting-free weighted sum rendering to bypass costly per-pixel depth ordering, and GEMM-based blending transformations that map alpha compositing to matrix multiplication, enabling the use of GPU tensor cores for faster blending and improved throughput.
2. Culling, pruning, and adaptive computation: Techniques to identify and skip Gaussians with negligible contribution to the current view using offline clustering and fast spatial indexing, and anisotropy that dynamically limits the number of Gaussian primitives processed per pixel or tile, reducing the per-frame computational load without affecting visual fidelity.
3. Multi-scale, level-of-detail, and progressive representation: A detailed look at hierarchy frameworks that build LoD by iterative selection or merging of Gaussian subsets for distance-based culling, floating point levels of detail to manage memory and quality, and progressive structures for flexible rendering on varying hardware capabilities, with techniques for seamless view-dependent refinement and LOD integration in large scenes.

### 4.1 Generalizable and Feed-Forward Gaussian Prediction
Description: This subsection examines the paradigm shift from per-scene optimization to amortized inference, where large neural architectures predict 3D Gaussian parameters directly from a set of input images in a single forward pass. It covers the architectural innovations—such as transformer-based encoders and pixel-aligned decoding—that enable zero-shot generalization to unseen scenes, alongside the inherent computational and representational trade-offs as input complexity grows.
1. **Transformer-based multi-view encoders and feature decoding**: The design of scalable architectures that consume multiple views, establish inter-view correspondences via self-attention and cross-attention mechanisms, and decode image features into dense pixel-aligned Gaussian primitives with attributes (e.g., position, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonics). These models typically remove the requirement of dense view inputs and circumvent the need for Structure-from-Motion initialization, enabling strong performance on object-centric and scene-level datasets.
2. **Spatially global, compact latent scene tokenization:** Novel alternations that first encode multi-view information into a compact set of global latent tokens—rather than unprojecting dense pixel-aligned primitives—to resolve cross-view conflicts and avoid redundancies. This facilitates a small Gaussian footprint, linear scaling with the added views, and enables a rapid, single-forward-pass procedure to generate a globally consistent representation.
3. **Orthogonality-based primitive selection and redundancy suppression:** Within the stream of feed-forward prediction, a strand of work has emerged to reduce primitive redundancy and improve reconstruction quality; this is realized via spatial sorting techniques that group spatially related Gaussians, geometric pruning in low-information regions, and adaptive resource allocation driven by scene complexity that ultimately improves both storage efficiency and rendering speed.
4. **Cross-task synergy between Gaussian generation and multi-view depth recovery:** A suite of methods highlighting joint, mutually beneficial learning for Gaussian prediction and depth estimation, typically by combining large monodepth priors with an explicit multi-view cost volume, directly informing high-quality feed-forward Gaussian prediction. Additionally, the thread of considering rendering as a form of multi-view supervision establishes a constructive pathway between self-supervised depth learning and geometry-aware rendering.
