lack of domain-specific knowledge, and outdated information. However, the

presents challenges for RAG systems. In response, GraphRAG leverages structural
information across entities to enable more precise and comprehensive retrieval,

current technologies is imperative. This paper provides the first comprehensive
overview of GraphRAG methodologies. We formalize the GraphRAG workflow,
encompassing Graph-Based Indexing, Graph-Guided Retrieval, and Graph-Enhanced
Generation. We then outline the core technologies and training methods at each
stage. Additionally, we examine downstream tasks, application domains,
evaluation methodologies, and industrial use cases of GraphRAG. Finally, we
explore future research directions to inspire further inquiries and advance
progress in the field.

- Interpretability and provenance tracing across the retrieval-generation boundary: There is a need for methods that provide end-to-end transparency, enabling users to trace how retrieved evidence influenced specific portions of generated output, which is critical for high-stakes applications requiring auditability.

- Robustness to retrieval failure and graceful degradation: Existing systems often assume high-quality retrieval; future work should develop techniques that enable language models to detect unreliable retrieval, express appropriate uncertainty, and gracefully degrade rather than confidently hallucinate when faced with irrelevant or misleading retrieved content.
- Efficient cross-lingual and code-switching retrieval-augmented generation: Multilingual retrieval-augmented generation remains underexplored, particularly in scenarios involving code-switching, dialectal variation, and low-resource languages where retrieval corpora and embedding models are limited.
