### 3.1 Surface-Driven Regularization and Geometric Prior Integration
Description: This subsection examines methods that impose geometric constraints during optimization to improve surface fidelity in 3D Gaussian Splatting. These approaches address the tendency of vanilla 3DGS to generate floaters and noisy geometry by integrating signed distance functions, depth priors, normal smoothness constraints, and cross-view consistency measures. The goal is to reinforce topological coherence and physical shape accuracy without sacrificing rendering quality.
1. Signed distance field and implicit surface alignment: Methods that couple Gaussian primitives with continuous SDF representations, using techniques such as Eikonal loss and projection-based consistency losses to enforce distance-field properties and align Gaussians to zero-level set surfaces.
2. Depth and normal prior regularization: Leveraging monocular depth estimation, multi-view stereo-derived depth points, or LiDAR data to constrain Gaussian positions during training, with techniques such as uncertainty-weighted depth-to-surface losses that suppress noise while preserving details in regions of boundary and high-curvature geometry.
3. Multiview and photometric-consistency constraints: Methodologies exploiting epipolar geometry, cross-view color consistency, and multi-view stereo depth to establish reliable geometry in color-variant regions, integrating MVS-based priors during initialization and optimization to prevent Gaussians from converging to false local optima.
4. Self-constrained and adaptive surface alignment: Iterative approaches that render depth from current Gaussian fields to derive TSDF volumes, constructing a self-updating, narrow band of the surface prior that guides Gaussian positioning, pruning, and opacity adjustments toward the refined surface estimate.
5. Rank-regularized and variational surface priors: Techniques addressing anisotropy and stability, such as regularization strategies to avoid needle-like Gaussians, promote disk-like shapes, and assign a globally coherent SDF-driven surface prior to guide all primitives to converge at the surface.

### 3.2 Surface-Adapted Primitive Kernels and Novel Basis Functions
Description: This subsection explores variants of Gaussian primitive kernels designed to better represent solid surfaces and capture sharp real-world edges. 1. 2D-Planar Gaussian radial and derivative kernels: Reformulations that collapse the 3D volume into oriented 2D planar Gaussian disks and surfels, including perspective-accurate splatting processes and ray-splat intersections, producing multi-view consistent and ready-to-mesh surfaces.
2. 3. Deformable and efficient kernel parameterizations: Learnable radial basis functions that allow adjustable angles, scales, and curvatures to account for anisotropic structures and complex features while enabling aggressive culling strategies and reducing the number of primitives for key rendering tasks.
4. Solid volumetric and Gaussian-mapped kernels for multi-view consistency: Begin with kernel functions that pursue multi-view coherency to avoid over-cycled fitting to artifacts, e.g., high-vs.-low opacity integration and mapping. Complementary approaches also use solid kernels to reduce transmittance errors and achieve higher consistency among 3D Gaussian positions.
