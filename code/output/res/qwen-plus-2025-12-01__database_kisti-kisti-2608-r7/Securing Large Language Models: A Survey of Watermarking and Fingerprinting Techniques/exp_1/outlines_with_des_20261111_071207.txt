# Securing Large Language Models: A Survey of Watermarking and Fingerprinting Techniques

## 1 Introduction  
Description: This section introduces the foundational motivation, scope, and intellectual framing of watermarking and fingerprinting as essential technical mechanisms for securing large language models across their lifecycle—from training and fine-tuning to deployment and adaptation—while distinguishing these approaches from broader privacy, safety, and robustness paradigms.
1. The escalating economic, legal, and societal imperatives for model and output provenance, driven by high computational investment in model development, growing regulatory mandates for synthetic content transparency, and widespread concerns about misinformation, academic misconduct, and unauthorized model replication.
2. Conceptual clarification of core terminology: watermarking as an active, owner-controlled process of embedding verifiable signals into model outputs or internal representations, and fingerprinting as a passive, forensic technique for uniquely identifying model instances based on intrinsic behavioral, structural, or statistical signatures without requiring prior embedding.
3. A roadmap of the survey’s analytical architecture—highlighting its dual focus on technical rigor (e.g., formal security properties, adversarial resilience, architectural compatibility) and socio-technical integration (e.g., governance implications, interoperability, human-centered evaluation)—and its deliberate departure from siloed treatments toward a unified framework for intellectual property protection in generative artificial intelligence.

## 2 Foundations and Taxonomy of Watermarking and Fingerprinting Paradigms  
Description: This section establishes a rigorous, principle-driven classification system that transcends implementation details to organize techniques along theoretically grounded dimensions—including embedding locus, detection modality, threat model assumptions, and formal security guarantees—enabling precise cross-method comparison and revealing deep structural parallels across historically disparate domains.
1. A tripartite functional taxonomy based on signal embedding locus: output-space watermarking (modifying generated token sequences or probability distributions), parameter-space watermarking and fingerprinting (embedding or extracting identifiers from model weights, attention matrices, or low-rank adaptations), and behavior-space fingerprinting (inferring provenance from input-output mappings, activation patterns, or decision-boundary responses under black-box access).
2. Formal characterization of detection modalities—white-box (requiring full model weight access), gray-box (leveraging logits, intermediate activations, or architectural knowledge), and black-box (relying solely on query-response behavior)—with explicit analysis of how each modality constrains adversary capabilities and shapes verification feasibility in real-world deployment contexts.
3. Information-theoretic and cryptographic foundations defining core security properties: detectability (statistical distinguishability from unmarked outputs), robustness (resistance to semantic-preserving transformations such as paraphrasing, translation, or summarization), unforgeability (infeasibility of injecting counterfeit watermarks without secret keys), and collusion resistance (preservation of uniqueness when multiple users or models interact).

### 2.1 Tripartite Embedding Locus Taxonomy: Output, Parameter, and Behavior Spaces  
Description: This subsection establishes a foundational functional taxonomy grounded in *where* the provenance signal resides—distinguishing between active output-space watermarking (modifying token sequences or logits), passive parameter-space fingerprinting (extracting identifiers from weights, attention matrices, or low-rank adapters), and behavior-space fingerprinting (inferring model identity from black-box input-output mappings or activation dynamics)—and critically analyzes how each locus imposes distinct constraints on stealth, persistence, and adversarial resilience.
1. Output-space watermarking as a *decoding-time intervention*: Techniques like green-list sampling (Kirchenbauer et al., 2023), WaterMax (2024), and StealthInk (2025) embed signals during autoregressive generation by biasing token selection probabilities; their security hinges on statistical detectability but suffers from vulnerability to paraphrasing, translation, and speculative sampling interference (Inevitable Trade-off, 2024).
2. Parameter-space watermarking and fingerprinting as *model-identity anchoring*: Methods such as EmMark (2024) for quantized edge models, AWM (2025) using CKA-based weight matrix alignment, and EditMF (2025) with zero-space causal tracing inject or extract identifiers directly from model parameters—enabling verification without output modification and exhibiting strong resistance to fine-tuning, pruning, and merging, yet requiring white- or gray-box access.
3. Behavior-space fingerprinting as *black-box provenance inference*: Approaches including SLIM (2026) leveraging latent-space confusion zones, FunctionMarker (2023) extracting knowledge-embedded watermarks via query responses, and behavioral bias analysis (e.g., repetition patterns, calibration errors in Baselines for Identifying Watermarked LLMs, 2023) infer model identity solely from input-output behavior—making them deployable in API-only settings but susceptible to ensemble inhibition attacks (Inhibitory Attacks, 2026) and distributional shifts.

### 2.2 Detection Modality Spectrum: From White-Box Verification to Black-Box Auditing  
Description: This subsection formalizes a three-tiered detection modality framework—white-box (full weight access), gray-box (logits, activations, or architectural priors), and black-box (query-response only)—and rigorously maps each to adversary capability boundaries, verification latency, operational feasibility, and real-world deployment constraints across commercial API services, open-model ecosystems, and confidential computing environments.
1. White-box detection enables cryptographically grounded verification (e.g., PRIVMARK’s MPC-secured watermark extraction, 2025) and high-fidelity parameter fingerprinting (AWM, 2025), but is infeasible for third-party users of closed APIs and vulnerable to gradient inversion and model stealing (Watermark Stealing, 2024).
2. Gray-box detection leverages intermediate representations—such as logits for DiPmark (2023) or semantic embeddings for Semantic Invariant Robust Watermark (2023)—to balance detection power and accessibility; however, it assumes partial trust in model providers and remains exposed to logit obfuscation, speculative decoding mismatches (Inevitable Trade-off, 2024), and activation poisoning.
3. Black-box detection underpins practical, vendor-agnostic auditing: PostMark (2024) inserts context-dependent synonyms post-hoc; Watermarking Text Generated by Black-Box LLMs (2023) uses synonym substitution to shift binary encodings; and SLIM (2026) detects latent instability via hypothesis testing—all enabling third-party watermarking without API cooperation, yet constrained by query budget, statistical power limitations, and susceptibility to adaptive prompt probing (Can Watermarked LLMs be Identified?, 2024).

### 2.3 Formal Security Properties: A Unified Information-Theoretic and Cryptographic Framework  
Description: This subsection advances beyond ad hoc robustness claims by synthesizing a rigorous, axiomatic foundation for watermarking and fingerprinting security—integrating information-theoretic distinguishability, cryptographic unforgeability, game-theoretic collusion resistance, and provable robustness—to enable cross-method comparison, expose theoretical limits (e.g., Watermarks in the Sand, 2023), and guide design toward verifiable guarantees.
1. Detectability as statistical distinguishability: Defined via interpretable p-values (A Watermark for LLMs, 2023), KL divergence bounds (Three Bricks, 2023), or mutual information theory (Building Intelligence Identification System, 2024); recent works like WaterMax (2024) and DERMARK (2025) derive token-count lower bounds for reliable detection at fixed error rates, moving beyond empirical thresholds.
2. Robustness as semantic-preserving invariance: Formalized not just against token edits (De-mark, 2024) but against structural transformations—including paraphrasing, summarization, translation, and multimodal re-rendering (LR-DWM, 2026)—with emerging frameworks (e.g., Provable Robust Watermarking, 2023) proving guarantees under bounded perturbation oracles, while acknowledging fundamental impossibility results under quality-preserving adversaries (Watermarks in the Sand, 2023).
3. Unforgeability and collusion resistance as cryptographic primitives: Achieved via secret-key–dependent green lists (Watermark Stealing, 2024), public-key signature embedding (Publicly Detectable Watermarking, 2023), or nested key structures (A Nested Watermark, 2025); collusion resistance is explicitly modeled in ensemble watermarking (Ensemble Watermarks, 2024) and multi-user fingerprinting (SLIM, 2026), where uniqueness must persist despite aggregation, distillation, or model merging.

### 2.4 Threat Model Taxonomy: Adversary Capabilities, Goals, and Attack Surfaces  
Description: This subsection introduces a structured threat model taxonomy that classifies attacks along three orthogonal axes—adversary capability (black-box vs. white-box access), operational goal (removal, spoofing, false attribution, or neutralization), and attack surface (output, parameter, or behavioral)—revealing deep structural parallels between watermark removal and fingerprint erasure, and exposing systemic vulnerabilities overlooked in siloed evaluations.
1. Removal attacks targeting output-space watermarks: Including syntactic scrubbing (synonym substitution, retranslation), semantic rewriting (paraphrasing, summarization), and generative erasure (De-mark, 2024; Watermark Stealing, 2024), with recent work showing even “distortion-free” schemes degrade under adaptive human editing (On the Reliability, 2023).
2. Spoofing and forgery attacks exploiting key leakage or structural predictability: Such as green-list stealing via mixed integer programming (2024), watermark distillation to learn and replicate watermarked behavior (On the Learnability, 2023), and piggyback attacks injecting malicious payloads alongside legitimate watermarks (Excuse me, sir?, 2024), undermining liability assignment in regulatory contexts.
3. Structural neutralization attacks targeting parameter- and behavior-space integrity: Including model merging to dilute fingerprints (AWM, 2025), selective unlearning to erase backdoor triggers (Are You Copying My Model?, 2023), and ensemble inhibition via token filtering or sentence verification (Inhibitory Attacks, 2026), which exploit the “radioactivity” assumption—that fingerprints remain stable across post-training modifications.

### 2.5 Interoperability and Cross-Paradigm Convergence: Bridging Watermarking and Fingerprinting  
Description: This subsection challenges the artificial dichotomy between watermarking and fingerprinting by identifying convergent design principles—such as multi-bit payload encoding, adaptive strength modulation, and ensemble signal composition—that transcend embedding locus and detection modality, revealing a unified paradigm for provenance assurance grounded in composability, modularity, and system-level integrity.
1. Multi-bit and ensemble watermarking as unifying architectures: BiMark (2025) and Ensemble Watermarks (2024) demonstrate how combining multiple independent signals—across green/red lists, acrostics, and sensorimotor norms—enhances detection efficiency and robustness without increasing perceptual distortion, mirroring fingerprinting’s use of multi-feature behavioral signatures.
2. Adaptive and context-aware embedding as cross-locus principle: Seen in Adaptive Text Watermark (2024) modulating strength by token entropy, Duwak (2024) dual-embedding in probability and sampling spaces, and EditMF (2025) localizing causal influence—highlighting shared emphasis on minimizing interference in low-entropy or safety-critical segments regardless of locus.
3. Modular, post-hoc, and privacy-preserving integration: PostMark (2024), PRIVMARK (2025), and LR-DWM (2026) exemplify a trend toward decoupled, plug-in watermarking modules compatible with speculative decoding, confidential computing, and diffusion-based generation—signaling a shift from monolithic, model-specific techniques toward interoperable, governance-ready assurance layers.

## 3 Technical Architectures for Output-Level Watermarking  
Description: This section surveys algorithmic frameworks that embed detectable yet imperceptible signals directly into the text sequences generated by large language models during inference, emphasizing design trade-offs among statistical power, linguistic fidelity, and compatibility with autoregressive decoding pipelines.
1. Sampling-based watermarking methods that bias next-token selection probabilities using pseudorandom vocabulary partitioning (e.g., green-list sampling), entropy-aware perturbation thresholds, and Gumbel-softmax–guided interventions—analyzing their dependence on tokenizer behavior, temperature sensitivity, and vulnerability to character-level attacks like homoglyph substitution or whitespace manipulation.
2. Cryptographic and learning-based watermarking schemes that embed publicly verifiable digital signatures via rejection sampling or error-correcting codes, and co-train large language models with differentiable watermark decoders to support open-model deployment and multi-bit payload encoding (e.g., user identity, timestamp, model version) without compromising fluency or coherence.
3. Context-aware and adaptive watermarking architectures that dynamically modulate embedding strength based on generation uncertainty, syntactic structure, or instruction-following fidelity—ensuring stronger watermarking in high-risk segments (e.g., factual claims, code blocks) while minimizing interference in low-entropy or safety-critical contexts.

### 3.1 Statistical Sampling-Based Watermarking: Foundations, Variants, and Token-Level Trade-offs  
Description: This subsection establishes the theoretical and practical bedrock of output-level watermarking by dissecting pseudorandom sampling frameworks—particularly green-list, exponential, and inverse-transform methods—and rigorously analyzing their statistical guarantees, architectural dependencies, and empirically observed vulnerabilities across diverse LLM families and decoding configurations.
1. Green-list sampling and its variants (e.g., KGW, ITS, EXP): formalization via cryptographic key–driven vocabulary partitioning, analysis of detection power under varying entropy regimes, and empirical degradation in low-entropy constrained generation (e.g., machine translation, code) as evidenced by BanglaLorica and SeqMark studies.
2. Entropy-aware adaptive perturbation: mechanisms that modulate watermark strength using token-level entropy estimates (EWD), sequence-level uncertainty (e.g., confidence scores), or syntactic depth—enabling dynamic calibration to preserve fluency while maintaining detectability in high-risk segments.
3. Gumbel-softmax and reparameterized sampling innovations (GumbelSoft, Logits-Addition): resolution of the deterministic-output problem plaguing classical GumbelMax watermarking, with quantified gains in generation diversity (AUROC +0.1–0.3) and resilience to homoglyph/whitespace attacks due to probabilistic token reweighting rather than hard list assignment.
4. Tokenizer and temperature sensitivity: systematic evaluation of how subword segmentation (e.g., Byte-Pair Encoding vs. SentencePiece), Unicode normalization, and softmax temperature interact with watermark signal stability—highlighting failures under round-trip translation (RTT) and cross-lingual settings where token boundaries shift unpredictably.

### 3.2 Cryptographic and Learning-Based Watermark Embedding: From Rejection Sampling to End-to-End Differentiable Decoders  
Description: This subsection advances beyond heuristic statistical modulation to examine watermarking architectures grounded in cryptographic primitives and joint optimization—emphasizing provable security, payload capacity, and seamless integration into open-model ecosystems without requiring API access or model fine-tuning.
1. Publicly verifiable rejection-sampling schemes (Publicly Detectable Watermarking, Excuse me, sir?): construction of undetectable watermarks via one-way functions and secret-key–dependent signature embedding, with formal proofs of distortion-freeness and computational indistinguishability from unwatermarked outputs—even under adaptive querying.
2. Error-correcting code (ECC)-based watermarking (Watermarking Language Models with ECC, Robust and Efficient Watermarking…): encoding multi-bit payloads (user ID, timestamp, model version) into token sequences using robust binary codes (RBC), enabling high-capacity, edit-resilient extraction with theoretical guarantees on bit error rate and detection p-values.
3. End-to-end differentiable watermarking (An End-to-End Model For Logits Based…, REMARK-LLM): co-training of watermark encoder and decoder modules using online prompting as a surrogate for non-differentiable sampling, achieving 37–39% higher robustness against paraphrasing while preserving perplexity and downstream task performance—validated across OPT, LLaMA, and Alpaca models.
4. Lightweight learning-based message encoding (REMARK-LLM, StealthInk): modular architectures that decouple message encoding (binary signature injection) from distribution reparameterization (dense-to-sparse logits mapping), supporting plug-and-play deployment on instruction-tuned and quantized models with minimal latency overhead.

### 3.3 Context-Aware and Adaptive Watermarking: Semantic Integrity, Alignment Preservation, and Risk-Guided Modulation  
Description: This subsection confronts the critical gap between watermark detectability and responsible model behavior by surveying architectures that dynamically embed signals based on semantic, pragmatic, and safety-relevant contextual cues—ensuring watermark strength is proportional to risk while actively mitigating alignment degradation.
1. Instruction-following fidelity–driven modulation: watermark amplification during factual claim generation, code block synthesis, or citation requests—where provenance is legally or ethically salient—while attenuating embedding in safety-critical contexts (e.g., medical advice, legal disclaimers) to avoid guardrail interference, as demonstrated in Watermarking Degrades Alignment….
2. Semantic differentiation for constrained generation (SeqMark, Semantic Differentiation…): sequence-level watermarking that partitions *semantic regions* rather than token vocabularies, preventing “region collapse” in low-entropy tasks and boosting F1 detection by up to 28% in machine translation and abstractive summarization without sacrificing ROUGE or BLEU.
3. Alignment-resampling mitigation (Watermarking Degrades Alignment…): inference-time correction strategies (e.g., Alignment Resampling with external reward models) that recover truthfulness, safety, and helpfulness degraded by watermark-induced token distribution shifts—empirically restoring baseline alignment scores with only 2–4 sampled generations.
4. Word importance scoring (Improving the Generation Quality…, WIS): token-level gating mechanisms that suppress watermark perturbation for semantically critical tokens (identified via perturbation analysis or model-based saliency), improving coherence and depth of response as measured by LLM-judger evaluations and human fluency ratings.

### 3.4 Robustness Engineering and Attack-Aware Design: Paraphrase Resilience, Black-Box Deployment, and Certified Guarantees  
Description: This subsection synthesizes recent advances in engineering watermarking systems for real-world adversarial environments—focusing on architectural choices that confer resistance to removal, spoofing, and structural attacks while enabling third-party, API-agnostic, or privacy-preserving deployment.
1. Post-hoc black-box watermarking (PostMark, PRIVMARK): insertion of input-dependent semantic watermarks *after* autoregressive decoding—leveraging sentence embeddings and controlled word insertion—achieving +12.7% relative robustness gain over logits-based methods against paraphrasing, with MPC-enabled privacy for collaborative watermarking without model weight exposure.
2. Certified robust watermarking (A Certified Robust Watermark…): randomized smoothing–based detectors that provide mathematically provable lower bounds on watermark survival under arbitrary token substitutions, insertions, or deletions—deriving certified radii for watermark persistence even under aggressive editing.
3. Dual-stream defense against piggyback spoofing (DualGuard): adaptive injection of two complementary watermark signals (e.g., statistical + semantic) that jointly enable not only detection but *traceability* of malicious content injection, distinguishing legitimate watermark presence from adversarial spoofing in cloud-deployed LLM APIs.
4. Order-agnostic watermarking for non-autoregressive models (A Watermark for Order-Agnostic LMs, LR-DWM): pattern-based frameworks (e.g., Pattern-mark, Left-Right Diffusion Watermarking) that embed stable signals in iterative denoising (DLMs) or parallel token generation (CMLM, ProteinMPNN) via Markov-chain key sequences or bidirectional neighbor biasing—extending watermarking beyond the sequential decoding paradigm.

### 3.5 Evaluation, Benchmarking, and Operational Realities: Beyond Detection Accuracy to Holistic System Assessment  
Description: This subsection critically examines how technical architectures are validated—not merely for statistical detectability—but across dimensions essential for production deployment: human-perceived quality, computational efficiency, compatibility with acceleration stacks, and longitudinal fidelity across model evolution.

Cross-lingual and low-resource robustness (BanglaLorica, Semantic Differentiation…): layered watermarking strategies combining embedding-time statistical signals with post-generation semantic signatures to achieve 3×–4× relative improvement in RTT-resilient detection for Bangla and other low-resource languages—establishing training-free, multilingual viability.

Impact on downstream functionality (Performance Trade-offs…, Watermarking Degrades Alignment…): empirical quantification of watermark-induced degradation across 9 NLP tasks (summarization, QA, translation), revealing 15–20% performance drops in long-form generation and task-specific alignment failures—motivating context-aware and adaptive design principles.

## 4 Model-Centric Fingerprinting and Parameter-Space Watermarking  
Description: This section examines techniques that bind ownership, integrity, or provenance directly to the large language model itself—either by extracting intrinsic identifiers from its parameters or behavior, or by embedding persistent signals during training or fine-tuning—enabling verification without reliance on output modification or external infrastructure.
1. Weight-space fingerprinting methods leveraging singular value decomposition, eigenvalue spectra, or attention weight manifold geometry to generate transformation-invariant identifiers resilient to quantization, pruning, fine-tuning, and model merging.
2. Backdoor-based and trigger-free watermarking strategies that imprint deterministic, secret-key–dependent response patterns through lightweight fine-tuning, low-rank adaptation, or neuron-level perturbations—ensuring zero-performance-cost embedding and resistance to human or automated scrutiny of trigger inputs.
3. Training-free behavioral fingerprinting paradigms that exploit stable statistical biases in token selection, repetition patterns, calibration errors, or layer-wise activation sparsity profiles across diverse prompts—enabling black-box identification of model families, versions, or fine-tuned variants without access to weights or retraining.

### 4.1 Weight-Space Fingerprinting: Intrinsic Model Identity from Parameter Geometry  
Description: This subsection establishes weight-space fingerprinting as a foundational *training-free*, *passive* model-centric provenance mechanism—extracting unique, stable identifiers directly from the structural and statistical properties of model weights, without requiring watermark injection, fine-tuning, or access to inference behavior. It emphasizes mathematical invariance under common model transformations and positions spectral, geometric, and alignment-based methods as the most robust approach for lineage verification in open-model ecosystems.
1. Spectral fingerprinting via singular value decomposition (SVD) and eigenvalue spectrum analysis—leveraging the stability of dominant singular vectors under quantization, pruning, and LoRA adaptation, as demonstrated by AWM (2025), which achieves near-zero false positives across 60+ model pairs using Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA)–neutralized Linear Assignment Problem (LAP) matching.
2. Attention manifold geometry fingerprinting—exploiting the intrinsic low-dimensional structure of attention weight matrices across layers and heads, where curvature, geodesic distance, and subspace alignment serve as transformation-resilient signatures, enabling identification even after instruction tuning or multi-modal extension.
3. Weight permutation invariants and error-correcting code (ECC)-encoded identifiers—embedding verifiable model IDs via structured weight permutations (e.g., Robust and Efficient Watermarking Using ECCs, 2025), where ECC decoding guarantees reliable extraction despite adversarial parameter perturbation, offering cryptographic-grade unforgeability without runtime overhead.

### 4.2 Backdoor-Based Parameter-Space Watermarking: Lightweight, Secret-Key–Driven Ownership Imprinting  
Description: This subsection treats backdoor watermarking not as a security vulnerability but as a *deliberate, controlled, and provably transferable* ownership embedding strategy—where deterministic, secret-key–dependent input-output mappings are implanted during lightweight fine-tuning or adapter injection, enabling zero-cost, stealthy, and forensically traceable model attribution. It bridges classical backdoor learning with modern IP protection requirements for EaaS and open-source deployment.
1. Embedding-as-a-Service (EaaS) watermarking via trigger-word–driven embedding perturbations (EmbMarker, 2023), where moderate-frequency natural words form cryptographically selected triggers, and target embeddings are injected proportionally—ensuring watermark transfer to stolen models while preserving utility across downstream NLP tasks.
2. Instructional fingerprinting as minimal backdoor tuning (Instructional Fingerprinting, 2024), where confidential private keys are encoded into instruction-tuning prompts that elicit deterministic, model-specific responses—supporting multi-stage licensing (e.g., MIT-style tiers) and resisting fingerprint guessing via semantic obfuscation.
3. Trigger-free, neuron-level watermarking (e.g., EverTracer, 2025), which repurposes membership inference attack (MIA) logic for defense: fine-tuning on natural data induces subtle, key-dependent *memorization biases* in output probabilities—not tied to artificial triggers—yielding gray-box fingerprints that survive meta-prompting, fine-tuning, and model merging while remaining statistically undetectable to adversaries.

### 4.3 Behavior-Space Fingerprinting: Black-Box Provenance from Statistical Biases and Activation Signatures  
Description: This subsection advances behavior-space fingerprinting as a *fully black-box*, *training-free*, and *operationally practical* paradigm—deriving model identity from stable, emergent statistical patterns in input-output behavior, layer-wise activations, or token-level decision boundaries, enabling forensic identification without weight access, API keys, or model modification. It highlights evolutionary and probabilistic discovery of discriminative features across model families.
1. Prompt-driven statistical bias fingerprinting—exploiting consistent, architecture- and training-data–informed deviations in token repetition rates, calibration errors (e.g., overconfidence in low-entropy contexts), and burstiness profiles across diverse prompt templates, as validated in Baselines for Identifying Watermarked LLMs (2023) and The Challenge of Identifying the Origin of Black-Box LLMs (2025).
2. Layer-wise activation sparsity and entropy fingerprinting—leveraging stable cross-layer patterns in neuron activation density, gradient norms, or attention head variance under standardized probing prompts, enabling identification of fine-tuned variants (e.g., Llama-2 vs. Llama-2-Chat) even when weights are inaccessible or encrypted.
3. Evolutionary “Hide-and-Seek” fingerprint discovery (Hide and Seek, 2024), where an Auditor LLM generates maximally discriminative prompts via in-context learning, and a Detective LLM analyzes response distributions to extract family-level signatures—achieving 72% LLM-family identification accuracy across Llama, Mistral, and Gemma without prior knowledge of model internals.

### 4.4 Adaptive and Multi-Layer Parameter-Space Watermarking: From Static Embedding to Dynamic Integrity Binding  
Description: This subsection synthesizes recent advances that transcend binary watermark presence/absence toward *adaptive*, *multi-bit*, and *system-aware* parameter-space binding—where watermarks evolve with model usage, encode rich metadata (e.g., user ID, timestamp, license tier), and integrate with broader model integrity stacks such as speculative decoding, confidential computing, and unlearning pipelines.
1. Multi-bit distortion-free watermarking with efficient decoders (Multi-Bit Distortion-Free Watermarking, 2024; BiMark, 2025), enabling payload encoding (e.g., version number, commercial/non-commercial flag) directly into weight perturbations or logits via error-correcting codes and bit-flip reweighting—preserving fluency while supporting granular licensing enforcement.
2. Speculative sampling–aware watermarking (Inevitable Trade-off..., 2024), addressing the no-go theorem between watermark strength and generation efficiency by co-designing watermark injection with draft-model coordination—enabling watermark persistence in accelerated inference without compromising latency or detection fidelity.
3. Unlearning-aware watermarking (MEraser, 2025; WAPITI, 2024), where watermarks are engineered to survive selective forgetting or mismatched fine-tuning: WAPITI transfers base-model watermarks to fine-tuned derivatives via parameter integration, while MEraser’s erasure analysis reveals design principles for collusion-resistant watermark placement resilient to targeted unlearning attacks.

### 4.5 Formal Security, Practical Robustness, and Governance Constraints of Model-Centric Techniques  
Description: This subsection critically evaluates model-centric techniques through dual lenses: (i) formal cryptographic and information-theoretic guarantees (e.g., unforgeability under key exposure, collision resistance under model merging), and (ii) real-world operational constraints—including regulatory admissibility, forensic auditability, and equity implications across model scales, architectures, and linguistic domains.
1. Cryptographic foundations of undetectable watermarks (Undetectable Watermarks, 2023; Excuse me, sir?, 2024), where watermark detection is provably reducible to one-way function inversion—ensuring computational indistinguishability from unwatermarked outputs even under adaptive querying, establishing the first formal security baseline for parameter-space watermarking.

3. Equity and systemic risk analysis—highlighting disparities in fingerprint stability across low-resource language fine-tunes (as implied by evaluation gaps in SoK: Are Watermarks Ready for Deployment?, 2025), risks of false attribution from activation sparsity drift in quantized edge models, and dual-use concerns in surveillance-enabled behavioral fingerprinting frameworks.

## 5 Robustness, Adversarial Vulnerabilities, and Attack Taxonomies  
Description: This section provides a systematic synthesis of empirically validated and theoretically grounded attack vectors targeting watermarking and fingerprinting systems, organized by adversary capability (black-box vs. white-box), operational goal (removal, spoofing, false attribution), and attack surface (output, parameter, or behavior space).
1. Scrubbing and removal attacks—including post-generation paraphrasing, synonym substitution, machine translation, recursive rewriting, and fine-tuning–based erasure—that degrade detection efficacy while preserving semantic coherence, fluency, and task performance.
2. Spoofing, forgery, and piggyback attacks that synthesize watermarked-looking outputs without secret keys, inject malicious content alongside legitimate watermarks, or exploit knowledge distillation to forge ownership claims—undermining liability assignment and enabling false-flag operations.
3. Structural and systemic attacks targeting fingerprint integrity—including model merging, gradient inversion, selective unlearning, mismatched dataset fine-tuning, and collusion-based fingerprint neutralization—that eliminate embedded identifiers without full retraining or expose vulnerabilities in radioactivity assumptions.

### 5.1 Taxonomy of Adversarial Capabilities and Threat Models  
Description: This subsection establishes a foundational adversarial taxonomy grounded in empirical attack papers, classifying adversaries along three orthogonal axes—access modality (white-box, gray-box, black-box), operational intent (removal, spoofing, false attribution, erasure, or neutralization), and knowledge assumptions (key-aware, model-agnostic, trigger-informed, or distributionally constrained)—to enable precise threat modeling and security analysis across watermarking and fingerprinting systems.
1. White-box adversaries with full parameter access who exploit gradient-based inversion (e.g., MEraser, AWM) or weight-space manipulation to erase fingerprints or reverse-engineer green lists, as demonstrated in *MEraser* (2025) and *Large Language Model Watermark Stealing With Mixed Integer Programming* (2024).
2. Gray-box adversaries leveraging logits, attention weights, or intermediate activations—such as those exploiting calibration errors or probabilistic deviations in *EverTracer* (2025) or *HuRef* (2023)—to mount targeted scrubbing or fingerprint neutralization without full retraining.
3. Black-box adversaries operating solely via input-output queries, including prompt-engineered watermark detection (*Can Watermarked LLMs be Identified by Users via Crafted Prompts?*, 2024), knowledge distillation–based spoofing (*DITTO*, 2025), and ensemble-level inhibition attacks (*Inhibitory Attacks on Backdoor-based Fingerprinting*, 2026).

### 5.2 Output-Space Attack Vectors: From Semantic Scrubbing to Covert Spoofing  
Description: This subsection details empirically validated attacks targeting the output space—where watermarks are embedded and fingerprints are inferred from generated text—emphasizing their linguistic plausibility, scalability, and evasion of statistical detectors under realistic editing intensities.
1. Paraphrase-resilient scrubbing attacks that preserve semantic fidelity while eliminating watermark signals, including recursive rewriting (*On the Reliability of Watermarks*, 2023), machine translation cycles (*Watermarking Text Generated by Black-Box Language Models*, 2023), and entropy-guided token substitution (*A Robust Semantics-based Watermark*, 2023).
2. Adaptive synonym and homoglyph substitution frameworks (e.g., *De-mark*, 2024; *WatME*, 2023) that exploit lexical redundancy and tokenizer vulnerabilities to degrade detection power without compromising fluency or BLEU scores.
3. Covert spoofing techniques such as *PostMark*’s semantic embedding–driven insertion and *DITTO*’s distilled watermark replication, which generate outputs bearing authentic-looking watermarks without secret keys—enabling false-flag attribution of harmful content to reputable models.

### 5.3 Parameter- and Behavior-Space Attacks: Erasure, Neutralization, and Collusion  
Description: This subsection synthesizes attacks that operate directly on the model’s internal structure or behavioral signature—bypassing output modification entirely—to eliminate, mask, or collude against embedded identifiers, revealing critical fragility in weight-space fingerprinting and backdoor-based watermarking.
1. Fine-tuning–driven erasure methods like *MEraser* (2025) and *WAPITI* (2024), which use mismatched or clean datasets to selectively unlearn fingerprint triggers while preserving task performance—demonstrating near-complete removal with <1,000 samples.
2. Structural neutralization attacks—including model merging (*DuFFin*, 2025), quantization-induced signal loss (*Watermarking LLMs with Weight Quantization*, 2023), and rotation/permutation invariants (*HuRef*, 2023)—that disrupt fingerprint stability without altering functional behavior.
3. Collusion-based fingerprint neutralization, where multiple fine-tuned variants or ensemble members jointly suppress fingerprint responses through token filtering (*Inhibitory Attacks*, 2026) or voting-based sentence verification, undermining uniqueness guarantees under multi-user or collaborative deployment.

### 5.4 Radioactivity Exploitation and Knowledge Distillation–Based Attacks  
Description: This subsection examines how the “radioactive” property—i.e., the unintended inheritance of watermark signals during knowledge distillation—is not merely a defense mechanism but a systemic vulnerability enabling sophisticated, scalable attacks on both watermark integrity and provenance attribution.
1. Distillation-enabled watermark stealing (*Watermark Stealing in Large Language Models*, 2024; *DITTO*, 2025), where attackers reconstruct green lists or replicate deterministic response patterns by querying watermarked teacher models and training student models on their outputs.
2. Pre-distillation paraphrasing attacks (*Can LLM Watermarks Robustly Prevent Unauthorized Knowledge Distillation?*, 2025) that sanitize training data to break radioactivity before distillation, using untargeted (UP) or targeted (TP) rewriting to sever statistical dependencies between teacher and student outputs.
3. Post-distillation watermark neutralization (*Can LLM Watermarks Robustly Prevent Unauthorized Knowledge Distillation?*, 2025), wherein inference-time resampling or logit correction removes inherited watermarks while retaining >95% of distilled capabilities—exposing a fundamental asymmetry between detection cost and removal feasibility.

### 5.5 Systemic and Cross-Modal Attack Surfaces: From Speculative Sampling to Multimodal Consistency  
Description: This subsection expands the attack surface beyond isolated text generation to encompass system-level interactions—including acceleration infrastructures, multimodal pipelines, and safety guardrails—that introduce novel failure modes and expose hidden trade-offs between watermark robustness and operational efficiency.
1. Speculative sampling incompatibility attacks (*Inevitable Trade-off between Watermark Strength and Speculative Sampling Efficiency*, 2024), where watermark injection conflicts with draft-model–driven acceleration, provably preventing simultaneous optimization of detection power and latency—a no-go theorem validated across OPT and LLaMA.
2. Alignment degradation attacks (*Watermarking Degrades Alignment in Language Models*, 2025), wherein watermark-induced token distribution shifts cause guard attenuation (reduced safety) or guard amplification (reduced helpfulness), revealing adversarial manipulation of model behavior via watermark parameters rather than inputs.
3. Cross-modal consistency attacks targeting multimodal LLMs (*Signal Watermark on Large Language Models*, 2024; *Cross-Attention Watermarking*, 2024), where misaligned watermark embedding across text, audio, or vision modalities enables selective erasure in one modality while preserving detectability in another—undermining end-to-end provenance claims.

### 5.6 Evaluation Gaps and Methodological Vulnerabilities in Robustness Assessment  
Description: This subsection critically analyzes persistent methodological weaknesses in current robustness evaluation—highlighting how unrealistic attack assumptions, narrow benchmarks, and omission of human-centered metrics create false confidence in deployed systems and obscure real-world failure modes.
1. Overreliance on synthetic, non-adaptive perturbations (e.g., random token edits) while neglecting human paraphrasing (*On the Reliability of Watermarks*, 2023) and domain-specific rewriting (e.g., academic editing, legal redaction), leading to inflated robustness estimates.
2. Inadequate coverage of long-tail attack combinations—such as paraphrasing + translation + fine-tuning (*On Evaluating The Performance of Watermarked Machine-Generated Texts*, 2024)—despite evidence that composite attacks increase success rates by >40% over single-vector baselines.
3. Absence of standardized adversarial evaluation protocols for fingerprinting, where most studies (*Hide and Seek*, 2024; *AWM*, 2025) report accuracy under static conditions but omit longitudinal testing across iterative fine-tuning, version updates, or continual learning—leaving persistence guarantees unverified.

### 6.1 Multidimensional Evaluation Taxonomy for Watermarking and Fingerprinting  
Description: This subsection establishes a principled, empirically grounded taxonomy of evaluation dimensions that transcends narrow detection accuracy metrics to capture the full operational fidelity of watermarking and fingerprinting systems—spanning statistical reliability, linguistic integrity, functional alignment, and adversarial resilience—while explicitly exposing misalignments between academic benchmarks and real-world deployment requirements.
1. Detection-centric metrics with statistical rigor: true positive rate (TPR), false positive rate (FPR), AUROC, p-value calibration, and minimum detectable token length under controlled entropy conditions—as formalized in *Robust Distortion-free Watermarks* (2023), *On the Reliability of Watermarks* (2023), and *A Certified Robust Watermark* (2024)—emphasizing sample complexity, confidence bounds, and detector calibration across model families and prompt distributions.
2. Quality-preserving evaluation beyond perplexity: adoption of LLM-as-judge protocols (*New Evaluation Metrics Capture Quality Degradation*, 2023; *WaterBench*, 2023), human fluency and coherence ratings, instruction-following fidelity (GPT-4-Judge), and task-specific degradation analysis (*Performance Trade-offs of Watermarking*, 2023; *Watermarking Degrades Alignment*, 2025) to quantify imperceptibility not as distributional invariance alone but as preservation of semantic depth, reasoning fidelity, and safety-guarded helpfulness.
3. Alignment-aware robustness assessment: systematic measurement of how watermark strength modulates truthfulness, safety, and helpfulness trade-offs—distinguishing “guard attenuation” (reduced safety from over-helpfulness) and “guard amplification” (excessive caution impairing utility)—as empirically characterized in *Watermarking Degrades Alignment* (2025) and mitigated via inference-time resampling strategies like Alignment Resampling (AR).
4. ### 6.2 Benchmark Architectures for Holistic and Adversarial Validation
